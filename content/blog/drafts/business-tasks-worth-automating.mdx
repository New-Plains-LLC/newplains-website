# 25 Repetitive Business Tasks Worth Automating—and 8 You Shouldn't

The best business tasks to automate are frequent, standardized, measurable, and reversible. The worst candidates depend on unclear judgment, unreliable data, or decisions with serious financial, legal, safety, employment, or customer consequences.

This list is a starting point. Every workflow still needs to be evaluated in the context of the business, systems, data, and people involved.

## Sales and lead management

### 1. Lead acknowledgment

Confirm that a request was received and explain the next step.

### 2. Lead classification

Identify the requested service, location, urgency, and likely owner.

### 3. Missing-information follow-up

Ask for the specific fields needed before a salesperson can proceed.

### 4. CRM record creation and updates

Create records, normalize fields, and record communication history.

### 5. Follow-up reminders

Create tasks when a lead or quote has not received the required response.

## Customer communication

### 6. Inbox triage

Classify messages, detect urgency, and route them to the right queue.

### 7. Draft responses

Prepare a reply using approved knowledge while leaving the final send to a person when needed.

### 8. Call transcription and summaries

Turn calls into searchable notes, action items, and follow-up drafts.

### 9. Status notifications

Send consistent updates based on a verified system status.

### 10. Frequently asked questions

Answer routine questions from approved source material and escalate exceptions.

## Documents and data

### 11. Invoice and receipt extraction

Extract vendor, date, amount, category, and reference information for review.

### 12. Form processing

Validate required information and route submissions by type.

### 13. File naming and organization

Rename and place files according to a consistent standard.

### 14. Spreadsheet consolidation

Combine recurring files, normalize columns, and flag mismatches.

### 15. Document generation

Create standard letters, summaries, proposals, presentations, or reports from approved data.

## Operations and management

### 16. Weekly operating reports

Collect known metrics, prepare the report, and highlight exceptions.

### 17. Meeting agenda preparation

Create an agenda from previous notes, open tasks, and stated objectives.

### 18. Meeting action-item tracking

Extract owners and dates, then place approved tasks into the correct system.

### 19. Internal knowledge search

Answer employee questions from manuals, policies, procedures, and prior documents with source links.

### 20. Exception monitoring

Watch for missing reports, overdue tasks, unusual values, or failed workflows.

## Finance and administration

### 21. Payment reminder preparation

Identify accounts matching approved rules and prepare notices for review.

### 22. Expense categorization

Suggest categories using company rules and prior examples, with uncertain items flagged.

### 23. Reconciliation assistance

Match records and isolate differences for a person to resolve.

### 24. Recurring compliance checklists

Track evidence and due dates without making the compliance judgment itself.

### 25. Vendor-information collection

Request missing documents, validate basic fields, and maintain a completion queue.

## Eight tasks that should not be fully automated

### 1. Final hiring and termination decisions

AI may help organize information, but employment decisions require accountable human judgment and careful legal review.

### 2. Binding legal conclusions

Systems can retrieve documents or draft material. Qualified people must make and approve legal decisions.

### 3. Unreviewed financial commitments

Do not give an agent unrestricted authority to approve payments, extend credit, sign agreements, or change bank instructions.

### 4. Safety-critical decisions

AI should not independently make decisions that could put employees, customers, or the public at risk.

### 5. High-impact customer promises

Pricing exceptions, refunds, delivery commitments, and contract changes usually need defined approval limits.

### 6. Sensitive communications without review

Personnel matters, disputes, complaints, and emotionally charged situations need a person.

### 7. Processes built on untrusted data

Automation cannot repair data quality merely by moving the data faster.

### 8. Workflows nobody understands

If the business cannot explain the process, its exceptions, and the desired outcome, it is not ready to automate it.

## Use a simple qualification test

Before building, ask:

- Is the trigger observable?
- Are the inputs available?
- Is the desired output clear?
- Can success be measured?
- Can errors be detected and corrected?
- Is there a clear owner?
- Can consequential actions require approval?
- Is the expected value greater than the full cost?

## Pick one, then prove it

Do not start by automating 25 things. Choose one workflow with enough value to matter and low enough risk to learn safely. Establish the baseline, build the smallest useful version, keep a person in the loop, and measure it.
