# Build, Buy, or Use Managed AI Agents? A Small-Business Decision Guide

Buy an existing product when the workflow is common and the product fits it well. Build a custom system when the workflow creates real advantage or spans tools that standard software cannot connect properly. Use managed AI agents when the system needs ongoing operation, monitoring, and continued improvement but the business does not want to staff that capability internally.

There is also a fourth option: build collaboratively. A concierge model can help a company develop the system with its own team while gaining outside architecture and implementation support.

## The four operating models

| Model | Best fit | Main tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Buy software | Common, standardized workflow | Business adapts to the product |
| Build internally | Strong technical team and strategic workflow | Requires internal capacity and ownership |
| Commission a project | Defined workflow and clear handoff | Changes after delivery may require new scope |
| Use managed agents | Ongoing needs across changing workflows | Recurring relationship and cost |

## When to buy existing software

Start with the market. If dependable software already solves the problem, buying is often faster and cheaper than building.

Good candidates include standard appointment scheduling, payroll, basic email marketing, electronic signatures, and common accounting functions.

Before buying, ask:

- Does it fit the process without extensive workarounds?
- Does it connect to the systems we already use?
- Can we export our data?
- Can we control permissions and approvals?
- What happens when volume grows?
- Are the AI features included, usage-based, or separately priced?

The hidden cost is process compromise. A low-cost product becomes expensive when employees maintain duplicate records or manually bridge the gaps it creates.

## When to build internally

An internal build makes sense when the company has technical capacity, the workflow is strategically important, and ongoing ownership belongs inside the business.

The internal team needs more than coding ability. It must understand security, integrations, model behavior, testing, monitoring, documentation, and the operating process itself.

Internal builds tend to struggle when they depend on one enthusiastic employee with no time formally assigned to maintain them.

## When to commission a one-off project

A defined project fits a workflow that can be scoped, built, tested, and handed off.

Examples include:

- A document-processing pipeline.
- An internal knowledge assistant.
- A recurring reporting workflow.
- Lead intake connected to the CRM.
- A call-summary and routing system.

The agreement should state the inputs, outputs, systems, acceptance criteria, recurring costs, documentation, ownership, and post-launch responsibilities.

## When managed AI agents make sense

Managed agents fit companies that want an AI operating capability rather than a single completed workflow.

The service should include:

- A defined agent environment and approved tools.
- Monitoring and error handling.
- Credential and integration maintenance.
- Continued workflow development.
- Usage and cost management.
- Human escalation paths.
- Documentation and operating support.

## Where AI concierge fits

The concierge model is for businesses that want to build with outside help rather than hand the entire project to a vendor.

It can work well when:

- An internal employee will become the system owner.
- The team wants to learn while implementing.
- Priorities will evolve as the first workflows launch.
- The business needs architecture and troubleshooting support.

The result should still include documentation, clear ownership, and a working operating process.

## Use this decision scorecard

Score each statement from 1 to 5.

### Favor buying when:

- The workflow is common across many businesses.
- A product already handles most requirements.
- Speed matters more than customization.
- The company can adapt its process.

### Favor an internal build when:

- The company has capable technical staff.
- The workflow is strategically important.
- Internal ownership is essential.
- The team has time for maintenance.

### Favor a project when:

- The desired result is specific.
- Scope and acceptance criteria can be defined.
- The workflow is reasonably stable.
- The client can own the finished system.

### Favor managed agents when:

- The operation has an ongoing automation backlog.
- Workflows and systems change regularly.
- Monitoring and support must be continuous.
- The company does not want to build an internal AI team.

## Questions that prevent a bad decision

1. Is the workflow common or unique to our operation?
2. Does it create competitive advantage?
3. How often will it change?
4. Who will monitor failures?
5. Who owns the data, prompts, source, and configuration?
6. What recurring costs continue after launch?
7. Can the system be moved to another provider?
8. What happens if the current internal champion leaves?
