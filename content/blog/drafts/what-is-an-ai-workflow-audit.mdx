# What Is an AI Workflow Audit? Cost, Deliverables, and What Happens Next

An AI workflow audit is a focused examination of a repetitive business process to determine whether it should be automated, what the system would need to do, and whether the likely value justifies the cost. A useful audit should end with a decision-ready plan, not a vague list of AI tools.

## What counts as a workflow?

A workflow is a repeatable series of steps that turns an input into an output. It often crosses several people or systems.

Examples include:

- A web lead arrives, gets qualified, receives a response, and is assigned to a salesperson.
- Receipts arrive by email, get categorized, and are added to a monthly report.
- Data from several locations is collected, reconciled, and turned into a weekly operating report.
- Customer calls are transcribed, summarized, and routed for follow-up.
- Employees search manuals, policies, and prior work to answer a recurring question.

“Help us use AI” is not a workflow. “Reduce the time required to prepare our Monday sales report” is.

## What should an AI workflow audit examine?

A serious audit should answer six questions.

### 1. What happens today?

The current process needs to be documented as it actually operates, including workarounds, delays, duplicate entry, informal approvals, and the spreadsheet somebody keeps outside the official system.

### 2. What triggers the work?

Every automation needs a clear starting point: an email, a form submission, a scheduled time, a status change, a new file, a phone call, or another observable event.

### 3. What systems and data are involved?

The audit should identify the CRM, accounting system, shared drive, inbox, database, phone platform, spreadsheets, and other tools involved. It should also determine whether those systems offer reliable ways to exchange data.

### 4. Which decisions require judgment?

Some steps can be handled with fixed rules. Others may benefit from AI. A third category still needs a human decision. Good design separates these instead of forcing AI into every step.

### 5. What can go wrong?

The plan should address missing data, duplicate records, ambiguous requests, unavailable systems, incorrect AI output, expired credentials, and the point at which a person must take over.

### 6. What would make the project worthwhile?

The business needs a measurable target. That could be hours recovered, response time, fewer handoff errors, faster reporting, increased capacity, or a lower cost per completed task.

## What an audit should not promise

An audit is not proof that every step can be automated. It should not turn estimated labor capacity into guaranteed cash savings. Recovering ten hours of employee time does not automatically reduce payroll by ten hours. The value may instead appear as faster service, more selling time, fewer missed follow-ups, or additional work handled without adding staff.

The audit should also avoid recommending a platform before the workflow is understood. The right tool depends on the systems, volume, exceptions, security needs, and people involved.

3. **Commission a defined project.** A provider builds the specific system for an agreed scope and price.

## Who is a good fit for an audit?

An audit is most useful when a business can name a workflow that is repetitive, important, and frustrating but is not yet sure what to build. It is less useful when the real problem is an unresolved management decision, a broken underlying process, or data that nobody trusts.

The best first workflow usually has clear inputs, a repeatable output, enough volume to matter, and a human owner who understands how the work is done.

## Questions to ask before buying an AI audit

- Does the audit examine a specific workflow or only provide general recommendations?
- Will I receive a written deliverable?
- Will the proposed architecture use the systems I already have?
- Will the report identify human-review points and failure modes?
- Will I receive an implementation estimate?
- Do I own the plan?
- Am I required to hire the same firm for implementation?
